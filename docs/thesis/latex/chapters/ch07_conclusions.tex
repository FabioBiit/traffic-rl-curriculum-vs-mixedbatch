\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This thesis asked whether ordering training tasks from easy to hard
produces measurably different multi-agent driving behaviour than
presenting all difficulties at once, at equal budget, in a shared-world
CARLA environment trained with MAPPO. The answer is yes --- but not
along the axis where curricula are usually sold.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ordering did not accelerate learning.} All four campaign
        cells reached the same early competence thresholds at the same
        step counts, plausibly because dense reward shaping already
        provides learning signal on hard tasks
        (\cref{sec:sample-efficiency}, \cref{sec:ordering}).
  \item \textbf{Ordering selected what the policy became.} Under the MLP
        critic, curriculum produced a cautious, stalling-prone driver
        and batch a fast, collision-prone one; on the strict success
        criterion the trade resolved for curriculum by $+18.5$\,pp at
        evaluation, consistently across all scenarios
        (\cref{sec:final-performance}).
  \item \textbf{The effect is conditional on value estimation.} Under a
        GNN critic the in-domain regime effect vanished
        ($-0.1$\,pp) while the architecture rescued the batch cell and
        reshaped the curriculum cell --- a strong
        architecture$\times$regime interaction
        (\cref{sec:arch-axis}).
  \item \textbf{The portable benefit is generalization.} The only
        component of the curriculum advantage that replicated across
        both architectures is the margin on a town never seen in
        training ($+19.0$ and $+10.0$\,pp on Town05), against the
        coverage-implies-robustness intuition
        (\cref{sec:generalization}).
  \item \textbf{Class specificity and platform limits.} Pedestrians were
        largely insensitive to the regime, bounded by route feasibility
        rather than policy quality (\cref{sec:ped-limits}); and every
        cell showed late within-run erosion at fixed task level,
        independent of regime (\cref{sec:behavior}).
\end{itemize}

Beyond the findings, the thesis contributes the apparatus that made them
measurable: an equal-budget comparison framework with seed-paired,
byte-identical configurations; a gate-driven development protocol with an
explicit registry of promoted, rejected and knowingly-retained changes;
a documented account of how environment-validity defects can masquerade
as policy effects, with their detection signatures; and a variance
yardstick from same-seed replicates against which every claim is read.
In a setting where a single simulator bug moved the headline metric by
fifty percentage points, this measurement discipline --- not any single
number --- is what makes the comparison meaningful.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

In order of how directly each item extends this thesis:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Seed replication.} The design was cut from three paired
        seeds per cell to one for compute reasons
        (\cref{sec:campaign}). Re-running the $2\times2$ matrix at
        $n{=}3$ would turn the paired case study into distribution
        estimates and test the interaction's magnitude directly.
  \item \textbf{Explaining the erosion.} The level-controlled
        within-run degradation (\cref{tab:quartiles}) invites targeted
        ablations: freezing one class mid-training to isolate
        co-adaptation, and varying the entropy-annealing anchor to
        isolate the exploration schedule.
  \item \textbf{Pedestrian route generation.} With more than half of
        pedestrian routes under target on Town03
        (\cref{sec:ped-limits}), a feasibility-aware planner (deeper
        branch exploration, adaptive targets per spawn region) is the
        highest-leverage environment fix, and would sharpen every
        pedestrian-side comparison.
  \item \textbf{Architecture extensions on the critic.} The
        infrastructure already exposes attention-based encoders (GAT)
        and PopArt value normalization behind flags
        (\cref{sec:critic-encoders}); given the strength of the
        critic-encoder interaction, these are natural next cells rather
        than exotic additions.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive curricula.} The competence gate is a fixed
        two-threshold sequencer; performance-based curricula that adapt
        level shares continuously (e.g.\ sampling proportional to
        learning progress) would test whether the equilibrium-selection
        effect survives finer-grained ordering.
  \item \textbf{Reward--curriculum co-design.} The gate-driven reward
        tuning ran mostly on easy-locked configurations
        (\cref{sec:threats}); re-tuning key shaping terms on the full
        mix would quantify how much of the regime effect is mediated by
        reward calibration.
  \item \textbf{Scaling the frame.} Parallel task coverage was fixed at
        $3{+}3$ agents; scaling the shared world toward tens of agents
        --- where the GNN critic's relational structure should matter
        most --- turns the coexistence frame itself into an experimental
        variable.
\end{enumerate}
