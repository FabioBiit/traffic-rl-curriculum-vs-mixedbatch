\section{Preliminary Architectural Pilot (April 2026)}
\label{sec:pilot}

Before the correctness fixes of \cref{sec:bugs}, nine runs (April--early
May 2026) crossed critic encoders and training regimes in the
first-generation environment: seed 42, budgets of $1$M or $3$M steps,
44D-era vehicle observations, and the pre-fix route planner. They are
reported here as a preliminary study --- not because their comparisons
hold (they do not), but because they motivated the methodology of
\cref{ch:methodology} and provide this thesis's clearest cautionary
example about aggregate metrics. All nine logs pass the integrity checks
of \cref{sec:metrics}; \cref{tab:pilot} reports the recomputed
cumulative metrics.

Four observations, in decreasing order of certainty:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Vehicles sat at floor in every cell} (SR 0.2--3.9\%).
        At floor, the success metric has no room to express differences:
        whatever effect architecture or regime may have had is compressed
        below the noise. The pilot's architectural comparison on vehicles
        is therefore \emph{uninformative} --- the counterexample that
        motivates running the main campaign in the mid-range regime. The
        floor itself was later traced to the route-length defect of
        \cref{sec:bugs}.
  \item \textbf{Pedestrians learned} (SR 66--82\%): the platform was
        trainable for the simpler class even in this era, localizing the
        failure to the vehicle task rather than to the stack --- and
        illustrating why all reporting is per class
        (\cref{sec:metrics}).
  \item \textbf{A qualitative architectural signature.} Among the
        curriculum runs, the plain GNN-critic run (0425) shows the most
        defensive failure profile of the nine: the highest
        stuck$+$timeout (70.8\%) with the lowest collision (19.5\%) and
        offroad (8.6\%), versus 53.2/30.6/14.2\% for its MLP counterpart
        (0411). At floor SR this is hypothesis-generating at most;
        whether a regime-dependent architectural signature reappears on
        the healthy trunk is exactly question H3, taken up in
        \cref{sec:arch-axis}.
  \item \textbf{Cumulative aggregates can hide a collapse.} Run 0504 was
        flagged during training as having collapsed mid-run --- yet its
        \emph{cumulative} vehicle SR (3.9\%) is the highest of all nine.
        \Cref{fig:pilot-collapse} resolves the paradox: the run sustained
        a windowed SR of 10--14\% for roughly 500 episodes, then dropped
        abruptly to near zero around episode 1{,}100 and never recovered;
        the cumulative figure is entirely inherited from the
        pre-collapse phase. This lesson --- current competence must be
        measured on a window, not since birth --- directly shaped the
        windowed unlock metric of \cref{sec:regimes} and the quartile
        reporting of \cref{sec:metrics}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Sample Efficiency}
\label{sec:sample-efficiency}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{learning_curves}
  \caption{Windowed success rate (100-episode window) versus environment
  steps, all four cells. Important caveat: each curve is measured on that
  run's \emph{own concurrent level mix} --- gated for the curriculum
  cells, uniform for batch --- so vertical comparisons between arms are
  not like-for-like until the curriculum has unlocked all levels; the
  distribution-matched comparison is the final evaluation
  (\cref{sec:final-performance}).}
  \label{fig:learning-curves}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Early phase: indistinguishable take-off.}
All three completed cells climb at the same speed: the 100-episode
windowed vehicle SR first crosses 30\% after 137--140K steps and 50\%
after 162--167K steps in every cell --- a spread of a few percent,
far inside noise. The notable part is \emph{what} each policy crossed it
on: the curriculum cells on easy-only exposure, the batch cell on the
full uniform mix including \SI{100}{m} routes. At equal budget, early
success-rate take-off was not the discriminating factor between regimes
in this campaign.

\paragraph{Unlock timing (curriculum cells).}
Medium unlocked at practically the same point in both curriculum runs
(episode 842 of 3220, 26.1\%, MLP; episode 860 of 3293, 26.1\%, GNN).
Hard, however, unlocked much earlier under the GNN critic: episode
1{,}910 (58.0\% of training) versus 2{,}286 (71.0\%) for MLP --- the
latter consistent with the 70\%-budget pressure cap firing rather than
the competence gate (\cref{sec:regimes}), while the GNN cell reached the
hard gate on competence. The result is visible in the exposure shares of
\cref{tab:runmatrix}: the GNN-curriculum policy saw $\approx 40$\% more
hard episodes than its MLP counterpart.

\paragraph{After the take-off.}
From $\approx 0.5$M steps the MLP-batch curve settles visibly below the
two curriculum curves (\cref{fig:learning-curves}), which track each
other closely; all curves drift downward late in training as the
curriculum mixes harden (curriculum cells) and as pedestrian windowed
success declines --- the within-run dynamics are analysed in
\cref{sec:behavior}.

\paragraph{The primary contrast, MLP pair.}
On the distribution-matched final evaluation
(\cref{tab:eval-mlp}), the curriculum-trained vehicle policy completes
48.33\% of routes against 29.83\% for batch: $+18.5$ percentage points,
with the same sign in \emph{all four} scenarios ($+19.0$, $+20.7$,
$+15.3$, $+19.0$\,pp) and in cumulative training ($+11.0$\,pp,
\cref{tab:cumulative}). The margin is roughly two to four times the
same-seed replicate band of \cref{sec:iterations}, and its consistency
across scenarios, difficulty levels and measurement layers is what the
paired design can offer in place of seed replication
(\cref{sec:campaign}).

\paragraph{Two different ways to fail.}
The regimes produce recognizably different driving
(\cref{fig:failure-modes}). The batch policy is
nearly twice as fast (18.7 vs.\ 10.2 km/h in training) but converts that
mobility into contact: collision 17.3\% vs.\ 8.8\% and offroad 12.8\%
vs.\ 3.4\% in training, widening to collision 26.0\% vs.\ 10.4\% at
evaluation. The curriculum policy fails mostly by stalling
(stuck$+$timeout 38.3\% vs.\ 31.2\% at evaluation). Neither profile
dominates the other on every axis --- the curriculum trades speed for
safety --- but on the strict success criterion the trade resolves
clearly in its favour.

\paragraph{Pedestrians: no regime effect.}
Pedestrian success is flat between arms everywhere: 56.6 vs.\ 57.1\% in
training, 47.0 vs.\ 48.9\% at evaluation, and within $\pm 6$\,pp in
every scenario. The training regime, in this platform, is a
vehicle-policy variable; the pedestrian task appears insensitive to task
ordering. Pedestrian failure structure is taken up in
\cref{sec:ped-limits}.

\paragraph{The GNN cells at a glance.}
In training, the GNN-curriculum cell lands within noise of
MLP-curriculum on headline SR (56.55 vs.\ 57.28\%) while redistributing
the failure mass: less stalling ($-9.6$\,pp stuck$+$timeout), more
contact ($+4.5$\,pp collision, $+5.8$\,pp offroad), and much higher
speed (18.0 vs.\ 10.2 km/h) --- closer to the batch cells' mobility with
curriculum-level success. GNN-batch pushes the same tendencies further
(26.6 km/h, the fastest cell) while \emph{beating} MLP-batch on training
SR by $+3.1$\,pp and on evaluation by $+9.3$\,pp
(\cref{tab:eval-gnn}). The critic encoder, then, shapes the risk profile
and interacts with the regime; the full architecture reading is
\cref{sec:arch-axis}.

\section{Behavioral Analysis}
\label{sec:behavior}

\subsection{Within-run dynamics: erosion, not just mix-hardening}
\label{sec:erosion}

its early peak. For the curriculum cells this is partly mechanical ---
their concurrent mix hardens at each unlock --- so \cref{tab:quartiles}
separates the two effects: the top panel reports overall quartile
trajectories, the bottom panel the trajectory \emph{on easy-level
episodes only}, where the task is constant by construction.

The level-controlled panel shows that mix-hardening is \emph{not} the
whole story: easy-level performance erodes within every run, from
$-6.4$\,pp (GNN-batch, the most stable cell) to $-18.6$\,pp (MLP-batch).
The batch cells make the case cleanly --- their task distribution is
constant, so their decline is entirely within-run degradation --- and the
curriculum cells reproduce it on their easy slice. Pedestrians erode even
more (easy-level ped SR: $-31.8$\,pp in MLP-curriculum, $-16$ to
$-22$\,pp elsewhere). Two candidate mechanisms are compatible with the
data and cannot be separated at $n{=}1$: cross-class co-adaptation
(pedestrian behaviour degrades alongside and feeds the vehicles' hazard
channel, \cref{sec:obs-actions}) and the entropy annealing schedule ---
though the transition completes only at 83\% of the budget, inside Q4,
while the erosion begins by Q3, so annealing cannot be the sole cause.
The clean conclusion is architectural: under the GNN critic the erosion
is halved or better, whatever its driver (\cref{sec:arch-axis}).

\subsection{Four policies, two failure grammars, one exception}
\label{sec:failure-grammars}

Mean speed orders the four cells: 10.2 (MLP-curr.), 18.0 (GNN-curr.),
18.7 (MLP-batch), 26.6 km/h (GNN-batch); contact failures follow the
same order (training collision 8.8 / 13.4 / 17.3 / 17.6\%), while
stalling anti-correlates (stuck$+$timeout 30.5 / 20.8 / 23.6 / 22.8\%).
What does \emph{not} vary is the internal structure of stalling: across
all four cells, stuck terminations decompose almost identically
($44$--$47$\% loop-detector plus no-progress with low completion,
$39$--$42$\% loop plus no-progress at higher completion, the rest minor),
and mean no-progress steps span a narrow 216--253 band. The regimes and
critics change \emph{how much} a policy stalls, not \emph{how} it stalls
--- consistent with stalling being one attractor (the defensive
equilibrium of \cref{sec:iterations}) that training either escapes or
does not.

\subsection{The defensive equilibrium is not intrinsic to the curriculum}
\label{sec:defensive-case}

The slow-and-stalling profile belongs to exactly one cell:
MLP-curriculum (10.2 km/h, stuck$+$timeout 30.5\%). Its curriculum twin
under the GNN critic is nearly twice as fast (18.0 km/h) with a third
less stalling (20.8\%) at the same headline SR --- so the cautious
equilibrium documented during development (hazard gate keeping vehicles
at rest, \cref{sec:iterations}) is not a property of curriculum training
as such. It is a basin the MLP-critic run settled into and the GNN-critic
run avoided, plausibly because value estimation over structured per-agent
inputs prices the hazard channel differently. At $n{=}1$ per cell this
remains an interpretation; what the data establish is that regime alone
does not determine the equilibrium.

\section{Generalization: Town03 to Town05}
\label{sec:generalization}

The test scenario places the same policies on Town05 --- a squared-grid,
multi-lane layout structurally unlike Town03 (\cref{sec:carla-bg}) ---
for 100 episodes at \SI{80}{m} targets, with traffic as in training.
Three observations from the MLP pair (\cref{tab:eval-mlp}), and a fourth
from the GNN pair (\cref{tab:eval-gnn}); \cref{fig:town05} summarizes
the transfer picture.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{town05_bars}
  \caption{Vehicle SR: Town03 scenario average vs.\ Town05 test, per
  cell. The curriculum cells drop least; the GNN pair is
  indistinguishable in-domain (43.6 vs.\ 43.7\%) and separates by
  10\,pp on the unseen map.}
  \label{fig:town05}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The ordering survives the map change.}
Curriculum completes 37.0\% of Town05 routes against batch's 18.0\%
($+19.0$\,pp) --- essentially the same margin as in-domain. The
absolute drop from the Town03 scenario average is nearly identical for
the two arms ($-15.1$\,pp from 52.1\% for curriculum; $-15.8$\,pp from
33.8\% for batch), so the curriculum advantage is not a Town03
idiosyncrasy: it transfers whole to roads never seen in training.
Read against hypothesis H2, the broader early coverage of the batch
regime bought it \emph{no} generalization edge --- if anything the
relative drop is worse from its lower base ($-47$\% vs.\ $-29$\%
relative).

\paragraph{Failure signatures amplify off-distribution.}
Each arm fails on Town05 the way it fails at home, only more so: the
curriculum policy stalls (stuck$+$timeout 50.7\%, its highest anywhere)
while keeping contact low (collision 11.0\%, offroad 1.3\%); the batch
policy crashes (collision 37.3\%, its highest anywhere). Novel roads
stress exactly the axis each policy already leaned on.

\paragraph{Under the GNN critic, the advantage exists \emph{only} out of
distribution.}
The GNN pair sharpens the point. In-domain the two GNN arms are
statistical twins --- Town03 scenario-average vehicle SR 43.6\%
(curriculum) vs.\ 43.7\% (batch) --- yet on Town05 they separate by
$+10.0$\,pp (35.7 vs.\ 25.7\%), with the smallest Town05 drop of any
cell for GNN-curriculum ($-7.9$\,pp) against $-18.0$\,pp for GNN-batch.
Where the MLP pair showed a curriculum advantage that transfers, the GNN
pair shows one that only \emph{appears} in transfer: same-sign evidence
that task ordering buys robustness to novel roads, delivered through two
different signatures. The interaction is analysed in
\cref{sec:arch-axis}.

\paragraph{Pedestrians transfer easily.}
Pedestrian success on Town05 (62.7 vs.\ 63.0\%) exceeds both arms'
Town03 hard-level results by $\approx 30$\,pp --- the \SI{80}{m} test
routes are more feasible than Town03's \SI{100}{m} hard routes, pointing
again at route feasibility rather than policy generalization as the
pedestrian bottleneck (\cref{sec:ped-limits}).

\section{Architecture Axis: MLP vs GNN Critic}
\label{sec:arch-axis}

The secondary axis replaces only the critic encoder
(\cref{sec:critic-encoders}); actors, rewards, environment and budgets
are identical, so differences between the MLP and GNN columns of the
preceding tables measure how \emph{value estimation} shapes what the
same actors learn. Two readings follow: the architecture's own effect
within each regime, and --- the H3 question --- whether the regime effect
survives the change of critic.

\paragraph{The regime effect depends on the critic.}
\Cref{tab:interaction} condenses the campaign's central result. Under
the MLP critic, curriculum beats batch by a wide, layer-consistent
margin. Under the GNN critic the same contrast almost vanishes: $+2.5$
\,pp overall at evaluation --- inside the same-seed noise band of
\cref{sec:iterations} --- and exactly zero in-domain, where the two GNN
arms are statistical twins (Town03 scenario average 43.60 vs.\
43.67\%). The only component of the curriculum advantage that
\emph{replicates across both architectures} is the out-of-distribution
one: $+19.0$\,pp (MLP) and $+10.0$\,pp (GNN) on Town05. Hypothesis H3
is therefore supported in its strong form: the critic encoder changes
not just the size but the very locus of the regime effect.

\paragraph{Where the interaction comes from.}
The compression is produced by two asymmetric architectural effects, one
per regime. Within batch, the GNN critic \emph{helps}: $+3.1$\,pp in
training and $+9.3$\,pp at evaluation over MLP-batch, together with the
most stable within-run dynamics of the campaign (the only cell without
monotone quartile decline, and the smallest level-controlled erosion,
\cref{tab:quartiles}). This is consistent with the mechanism motivating
CTDE critics in the first place (\cref{sec:marl}): under a mixed
distribution with six interacting agents, structured per-agent value
estimation should help most where the learning signal is noisiest ---
which is the batch regime from step~0. Within curriculum, the GNN critic
reshapes rather than improves: it avoids the defensive equilibrium
(\cref{sec:defensive-case}), unlocks hard on competence rather than by
pressure cap (\cref{sec:sample-efficiency}), sees $\approx 40$\% more
hard episodes --- and converts all of this into mobility and contact
rather than success (evaluation collision 22.9 vs.\ 10.4\%, SR $-6.7$
\,pp vs.\ MLP-curriculum). Pedestrians show the mirror interaction:
the GNN critic lifts them under curriculum ($+12.0$\,pp at evaluation,
the campaign's best pedestrian cell at 59.0\%) but not under batch
($-0.3$\,pp).

\paragraph{The April signature does not reappear.}
The pilot's qualitative reading (\cref{sec:pilot}) --- GNN-curriculum as
the most \emph{defensive} cell, maximal stalling with minimal contact
--- inverts on the healthy platform: the current GNN-curriculum is the
\emph{least} stalling curriculum cell with the higher contact rates.
A qualitative signature measured at floor SR, in a pre-fix environment,
did not survive the environment corrections --- one more instance of the
era-comparability rule of \cref{sec:bugs}, and a caution against reading
architecture effects from broken task distributions.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
All of the above rests on one paired contrast per cell pair: the
interaction pattern is internally consistent across four measurement
layers and both agent classes, but its magnitudes carry the usual
$n{=}1$ qualification (\cref{sec:threats}). The $+2.5$\,pp GNN-pair
overall delta is explicitly \emph{not} claimed as a curriculum
advantage; the claimed finding is its contrast with the MLP pair and the
architecture-replicated Town05 component.

\section{Pedestrian Structural Limits}
\label{sec:ped-limits}

Pedestrian results must be read against a constraint that no training
regime can remove: Town03's sidewalk topology often cannot furnish the
nominal task.

\paragraph{The diagnostics are regime- and architecture-invariant.}
Across all four cells the pedestrian route-generation diagnostics are
statistically indistinguishable --- under-target routes 55.1--55.9\% of
records, too-short flags 7.9--9.2\%, respawn-sourced routes 13.4--14.1\%,
and route sources split $\approx$40\% crosswalk-bridged chains /
$\approx$46\% plain sidewalk chains. This invariance is the point: these
numbers are properties of the map and the planner
(\cref{sec:routes}), not of the policies, so they set a common ceiling
for every cell. The \texttt{route\_short} demotion guard, by contrast,
fires almost never (0.6\% of pedestrian records): degenerate short routes
are rare; routes \emph{shorter than nominal but above the guard} are the
norm.

\paragraph{Success collapses with target distance --- everywhere.}
Training pedestrian SR falls monotonically with level in every cell
(easy 70--83\%, medium 53--60\%, hard 29--44\%), and the
distribution-matched evaluation reproduces the gradient (easy 58--72\%,
medium 41--54\%, hard 27--40\%; \cref{tab:eval-mlp,tab:eval-gnn}). A
collapse that survives two architectures, two regimes and two
measurement layers, while the route diagnostics above stay constant, is
driven by the task geometry: at \SI{100}{m} nominal targets, more than
half of the generated routes cannot even reach the target length, and
the ones that do demand long feasible sidewalk chains that Town03 often
lacks.

\paragraph{The Town05 counterfactual.}
The test scenario makes the attribution concrete: on Town05 at
\SI{80}{m} targets, pedestrian SR jumps to 54--71\% in every cell ---
15 to 31\,pp \emph{above} each cell's Town03 hard result, on a map never
seen in training. If the medium/hard collapse were a policy limit,
unseen roads should hurt, not help; instead, a more tractable sidewalk
topology recovers most of the loss. The binding constraint is
feasibility, not generalization.

\paragraph{What remains attributable to policy.}
Within the feasibility envelope, policy differences are visible and
consistent with \cref{sec:final-performance}: walking pace spans
1.45--1.96 m/s across cells (all inside the comfort band), pedestrian
collisions are negligible everywhere (0.2--0.3\% --- pedestrians fail by
time and route, not by contact), and one cell separates from the pack at
evaluation (GNN-curriculum, ped SR 59.0\% vs.\ 47--49\% elsewhere),
a thread taken up in \cref{sec:arch-axis}. The methodological
consequence stands regardless: pedestrian medium/hard success rates are
not a clean measure of policy quality on this platform --- this thesis
therefore compares pedestrians only within level and across arms, and
route-generation repair is listed as future work
(\cref{ch:conclusions}).

\section{Findings versus Hypotheses}
\label{sec:findings}

\Cref{tab:hypotheses} closes the chapter against the hypotheses of
\cref{sec:rq}. Two things survive every cell, every layer and both
architectures: the curriculum's advantage on the unseen map, and the
regime's role as a \emph{vehicle}-policy variable that reshapes failure
composition. What did not survive is the folk expectation on both sides:
curriculum did not learn faster early, and batch's broader coverage
bought no robustness.

Stated as answers to the research question: task ordering \emph{does}
produce measurably different multi-agent driving behaviour at equal
budget --- but not where curricula are usually advertised. It did not
accelerate early learning; it changed what the vehicle policy converged
to (cautious-and-stalling versus fast-and-colliding), it left
pedestrians essentially untouched, its in-domain success advantage
exists only under the MLP critic, and its most portable benefit ---
consistent across both critic architectures --- is robustness on roads
never seen in training. Every quantitative statement above inherits the
$n{=}1$-per-cell qualification of \cref{sec:campaign}; the
interpretation of these findings is taken up in \cref{ch:discussion}.
